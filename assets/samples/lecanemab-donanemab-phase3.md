## Introduction  

The therapeutic landscape for early symptomatic Alzheimer’s disease (AD) now includes two monoclonal antibodies that target amyloid‑β: lecanemab (commercially Leqembi) and donanemab. Both agents have progressed to pivotal phase‑3 trials—**CLARITY‑AD** for lecanemab and **TRAILBLAZER‑ALZ 2** for donanemab. Stakeholders (clinicians, payers, patients, and regulators) seek concrete data on two critical outcomes:

1. **Magnitude of slowing of cognitive decline** (e.g., changes in Clinical Dementia Rating‑Sum of Boxes [CDR‑SB], ADAS‑Cog, MMSE).  
2. **Incidence and severity of amyloid‑related imaging abnormalities** (ARIA‑E for edema/effusion, ARIA‑H for hemorrhage).  

The evidence set supplied for this analysis does **not** contain the primary efficacy results or detailed ARIA safety metrics from either phase‑3 trial. The following sections synthesize what is documented, explicitly flag the gaps, and place the available information in context.

## Conclusion  

The current evidence base confirms that lecanemab advanced to a phase‑3 pivotal trial (CLARITY‑AD) and secured FDA accelerated approval for early AD, with phase‑2b data indicating a 9.9 % ARIA‑E rate at the optimal 10 mg/kg bi‑weekly dose. Real‑world data (2023‑2024) show ARIA incidence and severity comparable to the trial, reinforcing the drug’s safety profile in practice.  

In stark contrast, **no phase‑3 efficacy or ARIA safety data for either lecanemab (CLARITY‑AD) or donanemab (TRAILBLAZER‑ALZ 2) are present in the supplied documents**. Consequently, this report cannot quantify the degree to which either antibody slows cognitive decline, nor can it compare their ARIA risk profiles directly.  

Stakeholders should seek additional, peer‑reviewed sources that publish the primary outcomes of CLARITY‑AD and TRAILBLAZER‑ALZ 2 to obtain the required comparative efficacy and safety metrics.
