# The State and Growth Trajectory of EV Adoption in Nepal: 2020 to August 2026 and 2026–2030 Outlook

## Introduction

Nepal has experienced a rapid transformation in its transportation sector over the first half of the 2020s, characterized by surging electric vehicle (EV) imports, expanding charging infrastructure, and shifting fiscal policies. The transition is driven by a combination of national climate commitments—specifically its Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)—and evolving tax structures designed to incentivize low-emission transport. 

This report unpacks the current state of EV adoption in Nepal, constructing a timeline from 2020 to mid-2026 based on available import data and fleet estimates. It further examines the policy and infrastructure landscape, explicitly surfacing conflicting data points regarding sales targets and charging station deployments. Finally, it synthesizes the available evidence to project the 2026–2030 outlook.

## Historical Context and Early Baselines (Pre-2020)

To understand the growth trajectory of the 2020s, it is essential to establish the baseline of Nepal's EV fleet prior to the decade's start. As of March 2019, Nepal had approximately 34,000 electric vehicles, which accounted for roughly 1% of the total national vehicle fleet [11]. At this stage, the market was heavily skewed toward public transport and light utility vehicles; more than 80% of these EVs were three-wheelers operating as public vehicles [11]. Four-wheeler passenger EVs were still a nascent market segment.

## The Growth Trajectory: EV Imports and Fleet Size (2020–2025)

The period between 2020 and 2025 marked a structural shift in Nepal's EV market, transitioning from a predominantly three-wheeler fleet to a surging four-wheeler passenger market. 

Between fiscal years 2019-20 and 2023-24, EV imports in Nepal surged by nearly 290%, increasing from 1,255 units to 11,701 units [9]. Year-by-year import data reveals the velocity of this adoption:

In terms of cumulative fleet size, the rapid influx of imports led to a significant expansion of four-wheeler EVs on Nepali roads. By early 2025, the number of electric four-wheelers surpassed 20,000 units [12]. 

The market has also seen the emergence of dominant international players. In 2023, Tata Motors held a 38% market share in the Nepali EV market, indicating a concentrated competitive landscape [2].

## National Policy Targets and Fiscal Structures

### Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) and Sales Targets
Nepal's EV trajectory is tightly coupled with its international climate commitments. In its 2020 Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC), Nepal set a target for EVs to account for 25% of all private passenger vehicle sales and 20% of all four-wheeler public passenger vehicle sales by 2025 [11]. This was reiterated in its second NDC, which maintained the target of EVs making up 25% of all private passenger vehicle sales by 2025 [4]. 

Looking further ahead, the government of Nepal has established highly ambitious targets for the end of the decade. It aims for 90% of private passenger vehicle sales and 60% of public transport vehicle sales to be electric by 2030 [10]. This aligns with the second NDC's goal of 90% EV share for private passenger vehicles by 2030 [4].

### Fiscal Policies and Taxation
Government tax policies have fluctuated, attempting to balance EV incentivization with revenue generation. In 2023, the Nepali government increased customs and excise taxes on electric cars with capacities between 50 and 100 kilowatts by 5% and 10%, respectively. This resulted in a new rate of 15% customs tax and 10% excise duty for that category [4].

Moving into recent fiscal years, Nepal has maintained a progressive tax structure based on vehicle power capacity:
*   **Fiscal Year 2025/26:** EVs under 50 kW are subject to a 10% customs duty and a 5% excise duty. EVs between 50 kW and 100 kW face a 15% customs duty and a 10% excise duty [6].
*   **Fiscal Year 2082/83 (Nepali Calendar, approx. 2025/26):** The excise duty for four-wheeler EVs with 0-50 kW capacity is confirmed at 5% [5].

## Charging Infrastructure Development

The expansion of charging infrastructure has been a critical enabler of EV adoption, though reported figures vary significantly depending on the source and timeframe.

In 2023, the charging network was relatively limited, with just over 60 DC fast chargers and 300 AC chargers available across the country [4]. During this period, the Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) aimed to install one charging station every 60 km along the country's main highways and had successfully installed 45 stations in collaboration with the private sector [4].

By 2024, the Investment Board Nepal initiated a project to establish 400 fast-charging stations in densely populated areas and along highway service corridors [9]. 

As of early 2025, Nepal reported having over 400 public charging stations, including 62 fast-charging stations installed by the NEA [9], [12]. According to a single source, by mid-2025, this number reportedly surged to between 1,250 and 1,500 charging stations nationwide [10]. 

## Conflicting Evidence and Data Discrepancies

A thorough review of the available evidence reveals two significant contradictions that complicate the analysis of Nepal's EV trajectory. 

### Discrepancy 1: EV Sales Share vs. Policy Targets
A major contradiction exists regarding the market share of EV passenger vehicles. One source reports that electric vehicles accounted for 50% of passenger vehicle sales in Nepal in the "last fiscal year" (contextually referring to FY 2022/23) [4]. However, Nepal's national policy targets (the NDCs) aimed for EVs to constitute only 25% of private passenger vehicle sales by 2025 [4], [11]. 

If EVs already achieved a 50% sales share by 2023, the 2025 target of 25% would be effectivelyobsolete and significantly underestimates actual market performance. This suggests either a misinterpretation of the "sales" metric in the reporting, a localized anomaly in sales data, or an overperformance so dramatic that the national targets require immediate upward revision. Given that the 50% figure is a single-source claim [4], it should be viewed with caution, but the discrepancy highlights a potential misalignment between policy goals and on-the-ground market realities.

### Discrepancy 2: Charging Station Deployment Volumes
The data regarding charging infrastructure deployment contains severe internal inconsistencies:

1.  **Early 2025:** Over 400 public charging stations (including 62 NEA fast-chargers) [9], [12].
2.  **Mid-2025:** Approximately 1,250 to 1,500 charging stations nationwide [10].
3.  **2026 Master Plan:** The Ministry of Energy plans to install 500 EV charging stations by 2026 (200 in Phase 1 by 2025, 300 in Phase 2 by 2026) [7].
4.  **Early 2026:** Over 400 fast-charging points installed across 7 provinces, with a projection to exceed 600 units [6].

The most glaring conflict is between the mid-2025 figure of 1,250–1,500 stations [10] and the Ministry of Energy's 2026 master plan to install only 500 stations by 2026 [7]. If the 1,250–1,500 figure is accurate, the government's 500-station target would have already been vastly exceeded by mid-2025. Furthermore, the early 2026 figure of "over 400 fast-charging points" [6] is difficult to reconcile with the mid-2025 claim of 1,250–1,500 total stations, unless the 2025 figure erroneously conflated individual charging plugs/points with full stations. Given that the 500-station master plan [7] and the 400-point early 2026 figure [6] present a more coherent trajectory, the mid-2025 claim of 1,250–1,500 stations [10] is likely an overstatement or a misclassification of infrastructure units.

## The 2026–2030 Outlook

Despite data fragmentation, the available evidence points toward a continued, aggressive electrification trajectory for Nepal through 2030.

### Infrastructure Rollout
The government's infrastructure rollout is structured in phases. The Ministry of Energy has unveiled a plan to install 500 EV charging stations nationwide by 2026, with 200 stations in Phase 1 (2025) and 300 additional stations in Phase 2 (2026) [7]. Concurrently, the government plans to install 424 EV charging stations specifically on national highways under a master plan [8]. 

By early 2026, the NEA, alongside private stakeholders, had successfully installed over 400 fast-charging points across the 7 provinces, with a projection to exceed 600 units [6]. Furthermore, industry reports suggest Nepal boasts over 400 public charging points as of early 2025, with plans to double this number by 2026 [12].

### Market Share and Policy Projections
The most definitive outlook for the 2026–2030 period is anchored in Nepal's NDCs. The government aims for 90% of private passenger vehicle sales and 60% of public transport vehicle sales to be electric by 2030 [10], [4]. 

If the import growth rates observed between 2021/22 and 2024/25 (where imports grew from roughly 1,800 units to over 13,500 units) are sustained or moderately decline, and if the 90% sales share target is pursued, Nepal's four-wheeler EV fleet will grow exponentially well beyond the 20,000 units recorded in early 2025 [12]. The progressive tax structure maintained for FY 2025/26 [6] indicates continued government favorability toward lower-capacity EVs, which will likely drive mass-market adoption in the run-up to 2030.

## Conclusion

From 2020 to August 2026, Nepal's EV sector transitioned from a low-volume, three-wheeler-dominated niche to a rapidly expanding four-wheeler market. EV imports surged by nearly 290% between FY 2019-20 and FY 2023-24, culminating in 13,569 imported units in FY 2024/25 and a fleet surpassing 20,000 four-wheelers by early 2025. 

However, the evidence base is marked by significant contradictions. A reported 50% EV sales share in 2023 [4] directly conflicts with the national target of 25% by 2025 [4], [11], suggesting either a massive policy underestimation or a data reporting anomaly. Similarly, charging station counts vary wildly, with a mid-2025 claim of 1,250–1,500 stations [10] conflicting with a government master plan to reach only 500 stations by 2026 [7]. 

Looking toward 2030, Nepal's outlook is defined by ambitious NDC targets of 90% private and 60% public EV sales [10], [4]. Supported by a progressive tax structure and phased highway charging deployment, Nepal is on a high-growth trajectory. Yet, achieving the 2030 targets will require resolving current data discrepancies to ensure that infrastructure planning and policy targets are accurately aligned with market realities.
